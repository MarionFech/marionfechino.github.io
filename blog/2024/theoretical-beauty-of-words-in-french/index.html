During my projects, I had to create and adapt many features at different levels, from sonority score for letters to contextual effects on style features. One of the many drawbacks of working in French in the NLP community is the need for more tools to be developed for that language and for adapting many existing features.

For my project, beauty and aesthetics were at the heart of the subject, so it was imperative to calculate a theoretical beauty value for each word.

Beauty is a generic word; in a more precise notion, we could talk about the affective-aesthetic potential to define this theoretical feature.

Affectivo-aesthetic potential (AAP) is calculated based on two French word lists (adapted from Jacobs, 2017) for negative and positive AAP. First, each word of interest is compared to each word in the positive or negative list based on the VSM word similarity. This similarity is based on a cosine distance between a pair of words. Then, an average for the positive and negative list is calculated for each word of interest, and the final AAP is the difference between those two averages.

Here is the function to calculate the potential based on a dataframe containing all the words of interest in a column. The model is the vector space model that needs to be built and calculated before obtaining a list of embeddings.

The analyses performed were based on the French poetry corpus (FPC, developed during my PhD) joined to a corpus created for this article based on French literature (called here, French Literature Corpus, FLC). The FPC includes 346 collections (14,940 poems) from the 16th to the 20th century. The FLC is composed of about 5,000 books in French from the 15th to the 21st century (some books are not free of rights).
# We built a 2.29 GB vector space model (as presented by Mikolov, Yih, and Zweig, 2013) using Fasttext (Bojanowski et al., 2016). The VSM yields 851,420 words; it was built with 300 dimensions, using skip-gram, with a minimum frequency of 1, and is used here in its deterministic version. To evaluate the quality of the VSM, we used a graphic representation of several keywords, as was presented in Jacobs and Kinder (2020). The idea is that TNSE representation should show how different concrete words and their most similar associated words should be graphically separated in a VSM rich enough to distinguish them. We also used abstract words in a second test to see how they overlap or are distinct. It is tricky for a VSM to define abstract words because they may be more spatially intricate.

TNSE for graphic verification of the vector space model for the corpus of literature for concrete words and abstract words (based on Jacobs and Kinder, 2020).

The final AAP value is the difference between those two numbers. This difference is the key for AAP as the more potential difference, the higher the defined tendency, either positive or negative, and not as a potential excitation, both positive and negative.

Negative AAPs present higher potential, as there are only 5 beautiful words in the final list of the top AAPs. Those are the most charged and weighed words from the selection.
